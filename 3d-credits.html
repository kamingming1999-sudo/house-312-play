Room 312

A browser horror prototype inspired by our fan-film concept. The environment and furniture use 3D geometry. The Roomkeeper uses an original AI-generated photographic cutout on a curved animated surface, with a 3D silhouette. Its breathing and approach are animated from the image.

Built with Three.js 0.187.0, MIT license. View the library license. The wallpaper, floorboards, upholstery, fireplace brick and aged CRT plastic are original AI-generated photographic material textures. Rain, grime, small landscape prints and glass wear use procedural textures. The lightning above the courtyard uses an original AI-generated storm sky. Rain on the window and the delayed thunder are synthesized in the game. The house design study is an AI-generated image, not a screenshot of the live game.

The nightmare Roomkeeper v4 image was created by editing the earlier original cutout with built-in image generation. The current scare draws its unlit face in the foreground, holds it for one second during play and 2.4 seconds in preview, and keeps the underlying hunter’s normal occlusion. Original whispered hunt omens and a low vocal growl were made with the native Whisper voice and filtering. The framed-photo fake-out uses an existing AI-generated, fully clothed fictional adult glamour photograph. The earlier film's image plates were created with built-in image generation. The warning uses the stock system Samantha voice. Four original whispered phrases use the native macOS Whisper voice with filtering and fades. The impact reuses the existing CC0 recording, with synthesized breath, air, heartbeat and bass. Pause and mute apply to the sound layers.

The woman waving from the opposite window is a fictional adult character created with built-in image generation using the supplied waving-girl thumbnail as a visual reference. Her detailed face, worn nightdress and hair are a new transparent asset; the raised arm performs a repeating double wave in the 3D scene. The stationary stair watcher uses a separate transparent face extracted and reframed from the supplied second horror image with built-in image generation. It appears after the handwritten table clue is read and remains visible until clicked. Restart requires reading the clue again. These characters use photographic surfaces placed within the 3D environment.

The optional field-radio investigation takes inspiration from the reactive environments and evidence equipment advertised for Demir Games’ Give Us A Sign. The receiver interface, house responses, journal and timings in House 312 are our original adaptation. Its radio request and reply use original lines performed by the stock macOS Samantha and Whisper voices, with radio processing created locally.

Room-tone source: Door Smash 1 by qubodup, CC0. The game reuses the quiet warning track from the film edit.

Reference study requested for this design: Junji Ito imagery, Ju-on, and Outlast. These guided facial unease, concealment and physical texture; the Roomkeeper is an original generated figure.

The instruction-and-hiding gameplay was adapted after analysing xAcceptiion’s Suzy Says! clip. MOONBIT’s original game describes an interactive DVD twist on Simon Says. This build uses original Keeper orders, its own timings, and the existing Roomkeeper and house artwork. No Suzy Says video, audio or character art is shipped in the game.

Chapter II, Beyond the Room, continues into the domestic hallway and kitchen. The rotary telephone, folded key cloth, front-door hinge and porch use original 3D geometry and existing materials. Its phone message, key investigation and three-knock encounter are original. The watcher behind the transom reuses the separately supplied face cutout. Telephone bells, individual knocks and receiver hiss are synthesized locally with WebAudio; pause and mute stop them.

The current version uses tap-to-walk with furniture-aware routes. Tap the monitor to approach it, then tap its screen to interact. The TV has a curved glass surface, recessed bezel, aged charcoal casing and restrained grey broadcast text. Breathing, whispers, knocks, slow light dips, moving room details and brief Roomkeeper appearances build the haunting. The quiet-exit choice remains available.

The house uses original geometry for a fireplace sitting room, barricaded stairs, narrow domestic hall and side kitchen. The supplied Google image-search link did not expose its selected image; the redesign follows the visible abandoned country-house references rather than an exact clone.

Chapter II uses original modeled figures, an original photographic occupant study, a generated practical-effects portrait of the Listener in the keyhole, English synthetic voices and authored foley. The porch uses a public-domain field recording courtesy of Bryce Canyon National Park. Dawn Chorus recording.
